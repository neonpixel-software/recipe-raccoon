# Phase 5: AI-powered capture — typed text — history

The dated notes for Phase 5, moved out of [PLAN.md](../../PLAN.md) word for word (#217): checks on devices, issue fixes and TestFlight stages, in the order they were written. Older notes can describe a state a later note replaced; PLAN.md has the current status.

## 5.1

**"Type it" entry point**

Acceptance: typing a real recipe produces a correctly structured, editable result; saving stores it like any manual recipe.

On-device check (2026-09-25, macOS 26.6.2, M4 Pro, Apple Intelligence enabled): the flow works end-to-end in the app — a typed recipe lands correctly pre-filled on the review screen. Checked off 2026-10-02 once #76 was fixed and the samples re-verified (see "Resolved since #76" below). Originally left unchecked because extraction quality doesn't yet meet the acceptance bar: on long, chatty recipes steps leak into the ingredient list, units and amounts get invented ("egg", "pcs", "none"; a pinch → 0.01), and "100g"-style amounts don't parse (issue #76). Check off once #76 is fixed and the sample set re-verified on device.

Amounts since #76: the model copies quantities as written and `IngredientAmountParser` resolves them, covering `1/2`, `1 1/2`, `½`, `1 ⅔` and `100g` (value 100, unit g). Capture uses greedy sampling, because default sampling sometimes garbled quantities ("1±³" for "1 1/2"), and equipment goes in its own field (see #82 below). Verified on 2026-09-25 against the real model on two samples: every fraction line parses.

Descriptors since #76: the model uses them as units ("1 large egg" → unit "large") and prompt wording didn't stop it, so `IngredientDescriptors` drops any unit that isn't a known measurement (English, Spanish, French, German, Dutch) and removes leading size words (large, medium, small, extra-large, jumbo) from the name. Verified on device: "1 large egg" → 1 egg, "2 medium potatoes" → 2 potatoes, "1 large yellow onion" → 1 yellow onion. Colour and variety words stay in the name, because "brown sugar" and "green onion" are different ingredients.

Equipment since #82: telling the model to leave equipment out wasn't reliable (the loaf pan and skewer came back after two unrelated lines were added to the sample), so a recipe now has an Equipment list, and the schema gives the model an `equipment` field between ingredients and steps. The mapping drops equipment that isn't in the source text, and drops an ingredient without a measurement unit whose line or name matches an equipment item (ignoring case, accents and spacing), because the model sometimes lists a tool in both ("8x4-inch loaf pan" named "loaf pan"). The unit check keeps food the model also lists as equipment, such as "¼ cup butter" when a step says "butter the pan". An amount check doesn't work, because the model gives tool lines an amount of 1. It doesn't filter by a list of equipment words, which would drop real ingredients such as "wooden skewers for kebabs". Verified on device (2026-09-25): with greedy sampling, the zucchini sample's loaf pan and skewer, the banana bread's loaf pan and the pancakes' skillet and whisk all land in Equipment and not in Ingredients, and "8 wooden skewers for kebabs" stays an ingredient. With default sampling (3 runs each of the zucchini and banana samples) no equipment showed up as an ingredient. Putting `equipment` first or last in the schema made steps leak into the crêpe recipe's ingredients more often, so it sits in the middle.

Resolved since #76 (2026-10-02, macOS 26.6.2): the crêpe recipe no longer leaks a step, but with greedy sampling it had started looping ("une pincée de sel" ~100 times until `exceededContextWindowSize`), which failed the whole capture. `.maximumCount` guides on the ingredient (40), equipment (15) and step (40) lists stop a runaway list, and an ingredient line or step that exactly repeats the one before it is dropped. With the caps the model turned two durations into ingredients ("une heure" named "heure", "environ une minute de chaque côté" named "minute"), so an ingredient named after a unit of time (5 languages) is dropped. It also re-emitted the end of a step as a step of its own, so a step that matches the end of the previous step (whole words) is dropped too. Known limit: a real step that happens to equal the previous step's last words would be dropped; a step reusing words from the middle stays. Steps aren't checked against the source, unlike ingredients and equipment, because the model legitimately splits and rewords them. The model also misspelled "Olivenöl" as "Olivenäl", and the source check silently dropped that real ingredient; a one-letter difference in a 5+ character text now counts as the source text, which is used instead (not for digits, so "4 EL" never matches "3 EL"). Verified on device with the sample set committed in `app/docs/capture-samples/` (chatty banana bread, French crêpes, pancakes, tomato soup, German vinaigrette), run through the app's own `FoundationModelsRecipeCaptureService` twice with identical output: every sample has the right ingredients, units and amounts, equipment separate, no steps or durations among the ingredients, and the original banana-bread case (steps as ingredients) doesn't reproduce.

Prose recipes since #76: the model often leaves the unit empty on prose lines ("1/3 cup melted butter"), so the mapping reads it from rawText after the amount, or from a word amount ("a pinch" → unit pinch, no amount; it used to become 0.01). Ingredients that appear nowhere in the source text are dropped, which removed an invented "eau" from a crêpe recipe. The converse risk: if the model rephrases both the line and the name (say, translates "250 g de farine" to "250g de flour" / "flour"), a real ingredient is dropped, and the review screen gives no sign of it. The "exactly as written" guide makes this unlikely, and none of the on-device samples hit it. Verified on device (2026-09-25) with a chatty banana bread, a French crêpe recipe and a tomato soup. Steps leaking into ingredients (issue item 1) did not reproduce on these samples; the original banana-bread text wasn't kept in the issue, so it is unconfirmed rather than fixed.

Thousands separators since #216 (2026-10-24): commas before groups of three digits are read as thousands separators ("1,000 g" → 1000, "2,500.5"); a comma followed by one or two digits is still a decimal comma ("2,5" → 2.5), and anything else with a comma gives no amount. Three digits after a comma stay a decimal comma when a thousands reading can't be meant: after a leading 0 ("0,125 l" → 0.125), and with a unit of 400 g or ml or more, written after the number or in the Unit field ("1,250 kg" → 1.25, since no recipe needs a thousand kilograms). Not supported: a German three-decimal amount of a small unit ("2,500 g" meaning 2.5 g) reads as 2500, and a point as the thousands separator ("1.000 g", as German writes it) reads as 1, the same as before.

Not supported: the crêpe recipe's ingredient sentence ("Pour 15 crêpes il vous faut 250 g de farine…") came through as its first step. It isn't filtered, because real steps also list ingredients ("Add a 28 oz can of crushed tomatoes, 2 cups vegetable stock…"); the user removes it on the review screen.

Compound amounts since #216 (2026-10-24): the on-device model can't combine "1 cup plus 2 tablespoons" and returns a single wrong number (1 2/3 cups instead of 1 1/8), so capture reads compounds from the ingredient line itself (`CompoundAmount`): a number and a unit, optionally "plus", "+", "and", "y", "et", "und" or "en", then a number and a unit ("1 lb 4 oz" too). Two volumes or two weights are added up in the first part's unit (US customary sizes, since cup-and-spoon compounds are a US habit). A weight and a volume, or a unit without a known size ("1 tbsp plus 1 pinch"), give no amount rather than a wrong one, and the user fills it in on the review screen. "1 lb, 4 oz" counts too. These aren't compounds, so the model's amount stays: a line with an ingredient between the parts ("2 tbsp butter and 1 tsp salt"), a container and its size ("1 can 14 oz beans", the first unit has no known size), and one amount restated in the other system ("1 cup 250 ml milk", "1 tbsp 15 ml"), since every real compound keeps to metric or to US/imperial units. Known limit: an amount restated within one system is still added up ("1 cup 8 fl oz milk" reads as 2 cups); the parenthesised form ("1 cup (8 fl oz)") isn't a compound.

Source lines since #215 (2026-10-26): on the review and edit screens, an ingredient row shows the line it was loaded from under its fields when the fields don't say the same thing, so a wrong amount or a lost note can be checked without the original recipe: a compound amount, "butter, softened", "salt to taste", or a line the model couldn't split. A line that is just its amount, unit and name stays hidden ("2 cups flour" for 2 / cup / flour, also "½" for 0.5, "cups" for cup, "250 g de farine", an amount within 1%, a name without its accents). A plural name still shows its line ("2 eggs" for the name egg). VoiceOver reads it as "As written: …".
